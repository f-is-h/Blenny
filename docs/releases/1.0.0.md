### Out of the crevice

A blenny is a small fish that spends most of its life tucked into a crack in
the reef. It peeks out when there is a reason to, then slips back in. That is
the whole idea behind Blenny, and today it comes out in public for the first time.

Blenny is a native menu bar organizer built only for macOS 27. It decides which
icons stay in view, which wait until you ask for them, and which stay out of
the way entirely. It does this without ever touching your pointer.

### Every app gets a place

- **Visible** stays in the menu bar while Blenny is managing it.
- **Revealable** stays tucked away until you open a reveal session, then
  goes back when you close it.
- **Hidden** stays in the crevice, even during a reveal. You can still find and
  bring it back from Blenny's Organize window.

Policy follows the application, so an app with several icons moves as one.

### Arrange first, commit once

Open **Organize** and drag icons between the three lanes and into the order you
want. Visibility and order go into one draft that you can look over before
anything touches the system. **Apply** writes the whole draft as a single
change through one serial writer, instead of replaying moves icon by icon.
**Discard Changes** throws the draft away.

Changed your mind afterwards? **Undo Changes** reverses the latest Apply,
including its order.

### What Blenny will never do

- Move your pointer, fake a click or simulate a Command-drag.
- Ask for Screen Recording.
- Poll in the background. It acts when you do, or when macOS reports a change.
- Keep retrying a failed system write. It checks once, retries at most once,
  then stops and tells you.

The app is about 6 MB, written in AppKit and SwiftUI, with Sparkle as its only
bundled dependency.

### You can always get out

- **Stop** releases every visibility restriction right away and keeps your order.
- **Quit** does the same, and remembers whether to resume next launch.
- **Undo Changes** reverses the latest Apply.
- **Recover Changes** finishes an interrupted change from its recorded scope.
  When the system no longer looks the way Blenny expects, it fails closed
  instead of forcing a write.

### Getting started

1. Download `Blenny-1.0.0.dmg` below and drag Blenny into **Applications**.
2. Blenny is self-signed and not notarized by Apple. If macOS blocks the first
   launch, go to **System Settings → Privacy & Security** and choose
   **Open Anyway**. You do not need to disable Gatekeeper or SIP.
3. Grant **Device Control** when asked. For ordering, pick the exact
   **Menu Bar Layout File** that Blenny shows in its file picker.
4. Keep the apps you want to manage in `/Applications`.

Requires Apple silicon and macOS 27. Updates arrive through Sparkle and are
verified against Blenny's own EdDSA key. Automatic checks are off until you turn
them on in Settings. `Blenny-1.0.0.dmg.sha256` lets you check the download.

### Rocky patches

macOS 27 does not hand every menu bar item to a manager, and some corners are
still rough. While management is active:

- Clicking the Clock does not open Notification Center. Swiping left from the
  right edge of the trackpad still does.
- Now Playing, and extras without an app Bundle ID (Gaming, Wine and similar),
  can disappear. Stop or Quit brings them back.
- Apps run from outside `/Applications` can lose their menu bar identity.
- Siri, Time Machine and Control Center cannot be sorted yet. Clock and the
  system overflow arrow stay where macOS puts them.
- Multiple displays have not been tested.

The [README](https://github.com/f-is-h/Blenny#known-limitations) has the full
table with workarounds, and
[KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md](https://github.com/f-is-h/Blenny/blob/main/docs/KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md)
records what has already been tried. If you know a clean way around any of
these, please open an issue or a pull request.

### How we got here

Blenny went through 18 private milestones, from 0.0.1 to 0.13.0, before this
release. Each one has its technical spike, research notes and evidence
boundaries written down, and all of it is public in this repository along with
the full commit history.

If Blenny keeps your menu bar a little quieter, you can support it through

personal paths and full menu bar inventories.
